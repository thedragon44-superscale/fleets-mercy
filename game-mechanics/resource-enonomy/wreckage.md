Wreckage, Salvage, and Logistics Rules

1. Wreckage Composition on Destruction
When any unit is destroyed, its physical hull leaves behind a harvestable wreck composed of two distinct material pools:

Structural Metal: The base amount of metal recovered is directly proportional to the unit's dock weight class (1W through 5W). Heavier units yield significantly more structural metal than lighter ones.

Payload / Ammunition Residue: Whatever resources remained active in the unit's onboard payload or ammunition cache are preserved in the wreckage. The more ammo/payload the unit had stored when it died, the higher the volume of those corresponding resources (Metal, Isotopes, Plasma Gas, Graviton Cores, or Ore) available for salvage. (For example, blowing up a fully loaded Cryo-Flak Frigate with a full magazine leaves behind a massive pool of volatile gas and metal).

2. Supply Unit Exclusive Access

Exclusive Harvest Rights: Combat units, mining ships, and command vessels cannot harvest unit wrecks for internal cargo. Only Supply Tenders (3W) possess the specialized recovery gear and cargo bays required to break down and haul these tactical wreckage fields.

The Closed Loop: Supply Tenders must physically travel to the battlefield wrecks, extract the proportional metal and payload resources, and haul them back to processing hubs or command ships to feed the economy.
